What is renewable energy?

Renewable energy is power that comes from natural sources that keep replenishing themselves, such as sunlight, wind, flowing water, heat from the earth and plant matter. Unlike fuels that run out, these sources can be used again and again to generate electricity, heat and fuel for transport.

Why does renewable energy matter?

Cleaner air and climate

Generating power from the sun, wind and water produces far fewer harmful emissions than burning fuels.

Never-ending supply

Natural sources are replenished constantly, so communities are less dependent on fuels that can run out.

Energy independence

Local generation lets towns and countries make their own power instead of relying on distant supply.

New jobs and skills

Building, installing and maintaining clean energy systems creates work in many trades and professions.

How does clean power reach us?

Step 1: Capture

Solar panels, wind turbines and water turbines collect energy from sunlight, moving air and flowing water.

Step 2: Convert

Panels and generators turn that natural energy into electricity that can be used on the grid.

Step 3: Store and balance

Batteries and other storage hold extra power for the moments when the sun sets or the wind drops.

Step 4: Deliver

Power lines carry electricity to homes, schools, hospitals and factories, where smart meters help manage use.

Benefits of
renewable energy

Lower emissions

Clean generation helps cut the pollution and greenhouse gases that come from burning fuels.

Stable long-term costs

Sunlight and wind are free to collect, which can keep energy bills steadier over time.

Power for remote places

Small solar and wind systems can bring light and electricity to places far from the main grid.

Healthier communities

Cleaner air and quieter power plants support better health for people living nearby.
